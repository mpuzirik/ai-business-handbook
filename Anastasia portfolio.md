# Portfolio Entry — Week 1

During this week I collaborated with my team to establish the focus of our project concerning AI development in businesses. One of the key decisions we made relates to working with a particular SME category rather than trying to cover SMEs as a whole. Our choice was to investigate small marketing and advertising companies, specifically focusing on **Reklaame**, a company from Arnhem.

I assisted in the definition of the general business problem we should consider. Rather than examining the types of AI technologies that marketing agencies might be using, we decided to ask ourselves the important question of how generative AI can be used to enhance productivity while keeping creativity, originality and trust in clients. I believe that this distinction is worth mentioning because AI deployment has to be oriented toward business outcome instead of following the latest technology trends.

I also worked on drafting our research proposal **AIBS** and the product requirements document **AEL**. This gave me a more complete understanding of the interconnections between both modules. In AIBS, I defined what we should conduct research on, whereas, in AEL we focused on what platform we will need in order to gather, assess, convert all the information into the handbook.

One significant aspect I noted during the work process was that we do not have to presume that we know the issues of the selected SME. For instance, we believe that AI might help save time on various tasks – from researching and brainstorming to drafting content and managing client briefs, but these are only presumptions. We need to validate them with the help of desk research and SME visit.

My next task will be to find appropriate sources in Dutch and English on the topic of the adoption of AI in marketing SMEs and gather information necessary for making contacts and interviewing a professional agency. AEL workflow simple enough that a non-technical team member can use it.

# Portfolio Entry — Week 2

This week, our team focused on the second phase of the AI in Business initiative. We examined the geopolitical context of AI, specifically the roles of the US, China and Europe. In particular, our work on the handbook consisted of translating the information into something usable for our target audience of small marketing and advertising firms in the Netherlands. Instead of merely asking which country is at the forefront of AI, we sought to find out where the AI technologies the businesses rely on come from and what risks such reliance may entail.

My role this week consisted mainly in gathering and analyzing various opinions on global leaders in AI. In particular, I studied AI-generated articles discussing the US, China and Europe and analyzed their conclusions. I noticed that some articles sounded almost equally convincing even though they concerned completely different criteria and concepts.

# Portfolio Entry — Week 3

In the current week, our team carried on with the progress of the handbook on artificial intelligence for small marketing and advertising companies in the Netherlands. The primary theme was related to trust and safety of AI.

My main contribution was the work on **AEL Knowledge Architecture**, where I concentrated on determining how the platform should process different sorts of information, including project context, original research material, source appraisals, evidence and handbook drafts.

One of the major decisions I had to make was to opt for **evidence retrieving**, rather than letting the platform rely solely on AI-generated summaries. The platform should first find appraised evidence and retrieve the original material and its source. This keeps important claims connected to their original evidence and reduces the risk of AI-generated content being treated as proof.

Another important issue was the treatment of field-research information. We kept the safety boundary from our Technical Blueprint: raw interview recordings, consent documents and identifiable SME information remain in protected storage and cannot be sent to an external AI model. Only reviewed and anonymised notes can enter the AI-assisted research workflow.

By the time I wrote this reflection, we had not yet received the Socratic Tutor questions regarding the Technical Blueprint or feedback on the Knowledge Architecture. Therefore, I did not create any false feedback.

My main build output this week was the **first draft of the Knowledge Architecture and the first entry in our Decision Log**. This work helped me understand that knowledge architecture is not only about where files are stored. It also determines whether the platform can explain where an answer came from and whether a researcher can verify it.

# Portfolio Entry — Week 4

What did your team publish?

This week, our crew kept working on the handbook aimed at small media buying firms in the Netherlands. The recently developed page was dedicated to the topic of the gaps in knowledge of small enterprises and what the owner of a firm should know prior to making a decision for using AI.

The page tackled several, very practical, issues, such as the possible fields where AI could generate value, which information needs to be given to the AI, what sort of checks need to be done on the AI-generated output, and whether employees know how to use AI responsibly.

What did I contribute?

This week was important in terms of my contribution to the handbook page. I contributed significantly to the identification of the key topics that any small agency should know before adopting AI.

I used various sources that included Eurostat, the Dutch Data Protection Authority, the European Commission, and KVK Amersfoort. In general, the most significant aspect of this work was identifying the writing style that should be employed in the page and ensuring that the information is intended for the owner of an SME rather than just describing what AI technologies are. 

I focused on understanding the difference between asking ‘What AI tool should we use?’ and asking ‘What business problem we are trying to solve with AI?’ This kind of approach makes the page more applicable to our target audience.

What did the gates ask, and what did we change?

During the demonstration of our first desk research tool, we needed to provide an explanation of what our tool can currently do, why we consider it agentic, and what we need to work on in terms of improvement. This prompted us to consider how our research process can be linked to the platform itself.

The issue that should be outlined is that it is easy to create a piece of content using AI, but we need to demonstrate how we select evidence and check the resources. Thus, we had this issue in mind while preparing the Week 4 handbook page.

What did my build role produce?

In this week, I made my major contribution to the creation of the handbook page and realization of our research. I assisted in transforming the somewhat broad subject of AI. knowledge gaps into specific questions that are relevant to small marketing agencies.

This week helped me understand that an SME does not necessarily need more information about AI in general. It needs the right information for a specific business decision. The handbook should therefore help an owner understand the value, risks and requirements of AI before deciding whether a particular use case is worth adopting.
