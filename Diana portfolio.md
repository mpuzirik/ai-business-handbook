# Portfolio Entry — Week 1

During Week 1, I was not able to attend the session, so I did not directly participate in the initial team discussions. After the session, I reviewed the work completed by my team and caught up with our project focus, the AIBS research proposal and the AEL product requirements document.

Our team decided to focus on small marketing and advertising companies in Arnhem and Gelderland, with Reklaame as our initial example. The main question is how generative AI could improve productivity while still maintaining creativity, originality and client trust.

As the Tester, I started looking at our project from the perspective of how we can check whether our platform and its outputs work correctly. For example, I think it will be important to check whether AI-generated information can be traced back to its original source and whether important claims are supported by reliable evidence.

One thing I learned from reviewing the project is that we should not assume that we already know what the SME needs. We currently think that AI could help with tasks such as research, brainstorming and content creation, but these are only assumptions. We need to validate them through research and an SME visit.

At the moment, I still do not fully understand how the agentic platform will work in practice and how we will test its outputs. I would like to understand this better in the next weeks and make sure that the final workflow is simple enough for a non-technical team member to use.

# Week 2 Portfolio

This week, our team focused on the geopolitics of AI and compared information about the US, China and Europe. We compared three different AI-generated articles and looked at how they described AI leadership. We noticed that the articles used different criteria, such as AI models, investment, patents, research and infrastructure.

My role in the team was Tester. I focused on checking the reliability of the information and looking for differences and unsupported claims. I also paid attention to whether important information could be traced back to a reliable source.

One important thing I learned this week is that an AI answer can sound convincing even when the evidence behind it is weak or unclear. Comparing different sources helped me understand why we should not rely on only one AI-generated answer.

This week helped me understand my Tester role better. I need to check not only whether the final information looks correct, but also where it comes from and whether we can support the claims with reliable evidence.


# Week 3 Portfolio

Week 3 Portfolio

This week, our team focused on Trust and AI Safety and on developing the knowledge architecture for our platform. I worked mainly on the Trust and AI Safety handbook page and created a Tester checklist.

I researched and structured information about what data a small marketing agency should be careful about when using AI, when AI should only provide advice, and why human approval is important for certain decisions. I also focused on confidential client information and the risks of putting company information into external AI tools.

For the Tester part, I created a checklist to check source quality, safety, usability and AI transparency. I wanted to make sure that important claims have reliable sources and that the final information is understandable for a small marketing agency.

This week helped me understand that AI safety is not only about getting a correct answer. It is also about protecting information, checking sources and making sure that people remain responsible for important decisions.

My main output this week was the Trust and AI Safety handbook page and the Tester checklist. I will also use my Tester role to check the different agent versions developed by the team.
