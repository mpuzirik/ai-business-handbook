# Portfolio Entry — Week 1

During Week 1, I was not able to attend the session, so I did not directly participate in the initial team discussions. After the session, I reviewed the work completed by my team and caught up with our project focus, the AIBS research proposal and the AEL product requirements document.

Our team decided to focus on small marketing and advertising companies in Arnhem and Gelderland, with Reklaame as our initial example. The main question is how generative AI could improve productivity while still maintaining creativity, originality and client trust.

As the Tester, I started looking at our project from the perspective of how we can check whether our platform and its outputs work correctly. For example, I think it will be important to check whether AI-generated information can be traced back to its original source and whether important claims are supported by reliable evidence.

One thing I learned from reviewing the project is that we should not assume that we already know what the SME needs. We currently think that AI could help with tasks such as research, brainstorming and content creation, but these are only assumptions. We need to validate them through research and an SME visit.

At the moment, I still do not fully understand how the agentic platform will work in practice and how we will test its outputs. I would like to understand this better in the next weeks and make sure that the final workflow is simple enough for a non-technical team member to use.

# Week 2 Portfolio

This week, our team focused on the geopolitics of AI and compared information about the US, China and Europe. We compared three different AI-generated articles and looked at how they described AI leadership. We noticed that the articles used different criteria, such as AI models, investment, patents, research and infrastructure.

My role in the team was Tester. I focused on checking the reliability of the information and looking for differences and unsupported claims. I also paid attention to whether important information could be traced back to a reliable source.

One important thing I learned this week is that an AI answer can sound convincing even when the evidence behind it is weak or unclear. Comparing different sources helped me understand why we should not rely on only one AI-generated answer.

This week helped me understand my Tester role better. I need to check not only whether the final information looks correct, but also where it comes from and whether we can support the claims with reliable evidence.


# Week 3 Portfolio

I researched and structured information about what data a small marketing agency should be careful about when using AI, when AI should only provide advice, and why human approval is important for certain decisions. I also focused on confidential client information and the risks of putting company information into external AI tools.

For the Tester part, I created a checklist to check source quality, safety, usability and AI transparency. I wanted to make sure that important claims have reliable sources and that the final information is understandable for a small marketing agency.

This week helped me understand that AI safety is not only about getting a correct answer. It is also about protecting information, checking sources and making sure that people remain responsible for important decisions.

My main output this week was the Trust and AI Safety handbook page and the Tester checklist. I will also use my Tester role to check the different agent versions developed by the team.

# Week 5 Portfolio

This week, our team focused on AI Use Cases at SMEs. We explored how small marketing agencies could use AI for tasks such as social media content creation. We also considered how a company could evaluate whether an AI use case provides real value by looking at time, editing effort, quality, costs and client satisfaction.

My role in the team was Tester. I participated in preparing and improving the interview guide for Reklaame. We wanted to understand how the company currently works, how it uses AI, what challenges it faces and what it still needs to learn about AI.

After receiving feedback, we realised that our original questions focused too much on making existing tasks faster. We therefore expanded the interview to include changing client expectations, new business opportunities, employee skills and decisions the company might be postponing because of missing information.

During the Reklaame interview, all team members participated and asked questions. I was also involved in asking questions and responding to the interviewee's answers.

The interview helped us understand the difference between assumptions and actual business experience. Before the interview, we could only make assumptions about which AI applications would be useful for the company. By speaking with Reklaame, we could learn more about its current practices, needs and concerns.

From a testing perspective, this was important because the agent and handbook should support real SME needs rather than only the assumptions we made at the beginning of the project.

My main learning this week was that AI should not be evaluated only by whether it can complete a task. We should also consider the quality of the result, the amount of human correction required and whether the application solves a real business problem.

# Week 6 Portfolio

In the final week, our team focused on bringing together the AIBS research and the AEL platform. We prepared the final research materials, handbook pages, interview findings, agent demonstration and final CBI presentation.

My role in the team was Tester. I reviewed the project from the perspective of reliability, traceability, safety and usability. I also worked on understanding the complete agent workflow so that I could explain its purpose and limitations during the final assessment.

I did not develop or program the agent. Samsul and Maksim were mainly responsible for its technical implementation. My responsibility was to understand how the system worked and what we needed to check when evaluating its output.

By the end of the project, I understood the main workflow:

Ingest → Privacy Check → Translate → Appraise → Extract Evidence → Organise → Draft → Check → Human Review.

I learned that the different stages have different purposes. For example, the system processes a source, checks privacy requirements, extracts evidence and prepares a draft. However, the presence of automated checks does not guarantee that every claim is true. Human review is still important.

The feedback we received throughout the project helped me understand why source quality, consistent evaluation criteria and clear human decision points are necessary. The Reklaame interview also showed why field research matters: it helps us understand the real needs of a company instead of relying only on desk research.

The most important thing I learned was that testing an AI agent involves more than checking whether the final text looks correct. I need to consider where the information comes from, whether the evidence supports the claims, whether confidential information is protected and whether the output is useful for the intended user.

At the beginning of the project, I did not fully understand how an agentic tool worked or how its output could be evaluated. By the end, I had a much clearer understanding of the workflow and my responsibility as a Tester. I also learned that AI can support research, but it should not replace critical thinking, reliable evidence or human responsibility.
