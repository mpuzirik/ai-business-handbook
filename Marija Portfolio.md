WEEK 1
Defining the problem and the project
What did our team do?
In Week 1, our team chose small marketing and advertising agencies as our target group. We selected Reklaame in Arnhem as our case company.
We created the first Research Proposal and the Product Requirements Document. The Research Proposal explained what we wanted to learn. The Product Requirements Document explained what our research tool should help us do.
What did I contribute?
I took part in the discussions about the target audience, the main business problem and the research question.
I helped review and organise the Research Proposal and the Product Requirements Document. I tried to keep the project understandable for a non-technical business owner.
I also helped define the main idea: technology can support some tasks, but people should remain responsible for quality, privacy and final decisions.
What did I do as Product Manager?
My role was Product Manager. I focused on who the user was, what problem we wanted to solve and what the tool should and should not do.
I did not program anything. At this point, I did not yet understand clearly how the technical system would work.
What did I learn?
I learned that a broad idea such as 'companies should use AI' is not a good research question. We needed a more specific question about productivity, quality, originality and trust.
I also learned that the research output and the technical tool were connected, but they were not the same thing.
 
WEEK 2
Source quality and the Technical Blueprint
What did our team publish?
Our team published a handbook page about geopolitics and AI providers. The page explained that a company should not choose a tool only because it gives a good answer. It should also consider the provider, data handling, prices and the possibility of switching to another service.
We also improved our search strategy and Technical Blueprint.
What did I contribute?
I helped review the documents after the teacher's questions. I focused on whether our research method and our technical plan matched each other.
I helped discuss how we should judge sources more consistently and how the process should work for Dutch-language sources.
What feedback changed our work?
The teacher noticed that we had many quality criteria, but the scoring system was not clear. We created one shared rubric with five scored criteria and four extra checks.
The teacher also asked how someone could judge a Dutch source before understanding it. We changed the process so that translation happens before the full appraisal when necessary.
We also became more careful about bias. An official source can be useful, but it is not automatically independent.
What did my Product Manager role produce?
I checked whether the revised Technical Blueprint still followed the requirements in the Product Requirements Document.
I did not build the scoring system in code. My role was to understand why it was needed and make sure the final process remained clear and useful.

 
WEEK 3
Trust, safety and interview preparation
What did our team publish?
Our team worked on the handbook page about trust and safety. It explained what information should not be shared with external tools, when a person should approve the output and why employees need clear rules.
For the technical part, the team created the Knowledge Architecture. It explained where sources, evidence, drafts and protected interview material should be kept.
What did I contribute?
I mainly worked on the research and interview side. I helped prepare questions for Reklaame about workflow, current tool use, privacy, employee skills and possible opportunities.
I also worked on understanding why an original source, selected evidence and a draft should be kept separate.
What changed?
We made the privacy boundary clearer. Raw recordings, consent forms and identifiable interview information should stay outside the model-assisted workflow.
We also kept open questions visible instead of pretending that everything had already been solved.
What did I learn?
I learned that a draft is not evidence. A draft may be useful, but every important statement still needs to be checked against the original source.
I also learned that responsible use is not only about privacy. It is also about checking meaning and keeping people responsible.

 
WEEK 4
SME knowledge gaps and the mini-CBI
What did our team publish?
Our team worked on the handbook page about SME knowledge gaps. We looked at what a small marketing agency may still need to understand before using new tools.
The team also prepared a working version of the research tool for the mini-CBI.
What did I contribute?
My main contribution was preparing for the mini-CBI and learning how the research tool worked.
Samsul and Maksim were mainly responsible for building the tool. They showed the rest of us how it worked and explained the stages.
I focused on understanding the purpose of the tool and how it supported our research.
What feedback changed our work?
The mini-CBI showed that it was not enough to demonstrate buttons and features. We also needed to explain why the tool was built and how it supported the research.
It helped me understand the connection between the business side and the technical side of the project.
What did I learn?
I learned that our tool is different from a normal chatbot because it follows a clear process, saves the work and stops when a person must make a decision.
I also learned that everyone in the team needed to understand the tool, even if they did not write the code.

 
WEEK 5
AI use cases and the Reklaame interview
What did our team publish?
Our team worked on the handbook page about practical use cases for small marketing agencies.
We also prepared the first evaluation plan for the research tool and improved the interview guide.
What did I contribute?
My main contribution was helping to improve the Reklaame interview questions after teacher feedback.
Our first guide focused too much on saving time. We added questions about changing client expectations, missing knowledge, employee learning, junior staff and new services.
During the real interview, everyone in the team asked questions. We did not have strict interviewer roles.
What feedback changed our work?
The teacher said our questions were strong on use cases but weak on knowledge gaps and people.
We changed the guide so that the company could tell us what it did not know, what decisions it was delaying and how the work of employees could change.
This made the interview more open and less focused on confirming our own assumptions.
What did I learn?
I learned that a good interview should not push the person toward the answer we expect.
I also learned that the best use case is not always the most advanced one. It should solve a real problem, fit the company and create value that can be measured.

 
WEEK 6
Final integration and preparation
What did our team complete?
In the final stage, our team brought together the handbook pages, the research tool, the demo video, the interview transcript, GitHub and preparation for the final interview.
The technical evaluation showed that the tool had useful checks, but that a statement could still have a correct citation and number while changing the meaning of the source.
What did I contribute?
My main contribution was reviewing the complete project and preparing to explain it clearly.
I reviewed the Research Proposal, Product Requirements Document, handbook pages, Reklaame findings, the workflow of the research tool and the main feedback changes.
I also prepared my portfolio and checked that I did not claim technical work that I had not done.
What feedback changed our work most?
The Socratic feedback changed our process most because it helped us make the source-review method clearer and improve the Dutch-source workflow.
The mini-CBI helped us understand that every team member needed to explain the purpose and limits of the tool.
The interview feedback helped us ask better questions about clients, employees and missing knowledge.
What did I learn?
I learned that a tool can check citations, quotations and numbers but still miss a wrong interpretation.
I also learned that it is better to recommend a small supervised test than to make a strong claim without enough evidence.

 
FINAL REFLECTION
What did I learn about research?
Before this project, I mainly thought research meant finding information and writing a report.
Now I understand that research also means checking who produced the information, what it really supports, what its limits are and whether it can answer the business question.
I also understand the difference between desk research and field research. Desk research gave us the wider context. The Reklaame interview showed what was happening inside one real company.
What did I learn about the research tool?
I did not program the tool, but I learned how it works and why the process matters.
The tool can help organise sources, preserve quotations, prepare drafts and save decisions. It cannot prove that every statement is true.
I would trust it as support for a researcher, but not as a system that can publish work by itself.
What did I learn about Reklaame and marketing agencies?
Reklaame already uses different tools for proposals, design work and blog content.
The interview showed that the company sees the biggest value in faster execution and easier growth.
At the same time, ideas, client relationships and final professional decisions still need people.
The main lesson is that agencies should use technology where it removes unnecessary work, but they should not give away responsibility.
How did I grow personally?
In Week 1, I did not understand how the research tool worked and I found the technical part difficult.
By the end, I could explain the purpose of the tool, the main stages, the role of human review and its important limitations.
I also became more confident in reviewing documents, preparing interview questions and explaining the project in simple business language.
What was my real contribution?
My role was Product Manager. I did not write the code.
My contribution was mainly on the research and product side: helping define the problem and user, reviewing the Research Proposal and Product Requirements Document, working on handbook content, improving the interview guide, participating in the interview and reviewing the final project.
Samsul and Maksim were mainly responsible for the technical build, and they explained it to the rest of the team.
My final view
I now see technology as something that can make work faster, but only when the company has a clear problem, good information, clear rules and a person who remains responsible.
The most useful recommendation from our project is to start small, measure the full process and expand only when the result is genuinely better.
