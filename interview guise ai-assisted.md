# SME Interview Guide - Reklaame

## Purpose of the interview

The purpose of this interview is to understand how Reklaame currently works, how AI is already being used, where AI could realistically create value, and what risks or limitations would need to be managed.

The interview is intended to support our research question:

**How can small marketing and advertising agencies in the Netherlands use generative AI to improve productivity while maintaining creative quality, originality and client trust?**

The interview focuses on Reklaame as one case company. The answers will not be treated as representative of the whole marketing sector.

---

## Before the interview

Before starting:

- confirm that the participant has received the consent information;
- confirm that participation is voluntary;
- explain how the information will be used;
- explain whether notes or recordings will be taken;
- confirm how the participant and company will be anonymised if required;
- do not ask the participant to share confidential client information.

### Opening script

Thank you for taking the time to speak with us.

We are researching how small marketing and advertising agencies can use generative AI in a practical and responsible way.

We are interested in how your work is currently organised, whether AI is already being used, which tasks take the most time, what concerns you have, and which AI applications might realistically be useful.

There are no right or wrong answers. We are interested in your actual experience.

Please do not share confidential client information, passwords, personal data or anything you are not comfortable discussing.

Is it okay if we begin?

---

# 1. Company and Role

### Question 1
Could you briefly explain your role at Reklaame and what you are mainly responsible for?

**Possible follow-up questions:**
- How long have you worked in this role?
- Which parts of the company do you work with most often?

### Question 2
What are the main services Reklaame provides to clients?

**Possible follow-ups:**
- Which services take up most of the team's time?
- Which services are most important commercially?

---

# 2. Current Workflow and Time

### Question 3
Can you walk us through a typical project, from receiving a client brief to delivering the final work?

**Possible follow-ups:**
- Who is involved at each stage?
- Where are the main handovers?
- Which parts usually take the longest?

### Question 4
Which tasks currently require the most employee time?

**Possible follow-ups:**
- research?
- brainstorming?
- writing?
- design?
- revisions?
- communication with clients?
- reporting or administration?

### Question 5
Are there any repetitive tasks that employees regularly have to do manually?

**Possible follow-ups:**
- Which of these are frustrating?
- Which would you most like to make faster?

---

# 3. Current AI Use

### Question 6
Have you or your colleagues used AI tools for work recently?

Instead of only asking which tools are known, we want to understand actual use.

**Possible follow-ups:**
- What did you use AI for recently?
- Which tool did you use?
- What was the task?
- How did you use the output?

### Question 7
Can you give us a specific example of something you produced with AI?

If appropriate and the participant is comfortable:

- What was the original task?
- What did the AI produce?
- What did you have to change?
- Was the final result actually useful?

Do not ask them to show confidential client material.

### Question 8
Which AI tools have been tried but were not useful?

**Possible follow-ups:**
- What went wrong?
- Was the problem quality, accuracy, usability, cost or something else?

---

# 4. Quality and Creative Work

### Question 9
Which parts of your work do you think AI could support without damaging creative quality?

**Possible follow-ups:**
- first drafts?
- idea generation?
- research?
- variations?
- summarising?
- administrative work?

### Question 10
Which parts of the work should, in your opinion, remain mainly human?

**Possible follow-ups:**
- Why?
- Is originality a concern?
- Is brand understanding important?
- Is client communication different?

### Question 11
When you see AI-generated marketing content, what usually makes it feel good or bad?

**Possible follow-ups:**
- originality?
- tone of voice?
- accuracy?
- creativity?
- understanding of the client?

---

# 5. Accuracy and Trust

### Question 12
How serious would it be if an AI tool produced incorrect information in your work?

**Possible follow-ups:**
- What types of mistakes would matter most?
- Who would normally check AI-generated content?
- Would different types of work require different levels of checking?

### Question 13
What would make you trust an AI-generated output enough to use it in client work?

**Possible follow-ups:**
- human review?
- source citations?
- testing?
- approved tools?
- company guidelines?

---

# 6. Privacy, Copyright and Client Information

### Question 14
Do you have concerns about putting client or company information into AI tools?

**Possible follow-ups:**
- What kinds of information would you never put into an AI tool?
- Do clients have rules or NDAs that affect this?
- Are employees currently given guidance on this?

### Question 15
How important is it to know where an AI provider stores or processes your data?

**Possible follow-ups:**
- Would an EU-based provider make a difference?
- Would you choose a less capable tool if it offered better privacy or data control?

### Question 16
Are copyright or ownership questions important when using AI-generated text, images or designs?

**Possible follow-ups:**
- Have clients ever asked about this?
- Would you want employees to disclose when AI was used?

---

# 7. Vendor Dependency and Tool Choice

### Question 17
What would happen if an AI tool your team depended on suddenly became more expensive or unavailable?

**Possible follow-ups:**
- Could you easily switch?
- Would important prompts, files or workflows be lost?
- Do you normally keep work outside the AI tool as well?

### Question 18
What matters most when choosing an AI tool?

Ask the participant to explain rather than simply select from a list.

Possible factors:

- output quality;
- price;
- ease of use;
- privacy;
- data location;
- integration with existing tools;
- language support;
- reliability;
- ability to export data;
- provider reputation.

### Question 19
Would you realistically consider using a European AI provider instead of a U.S.-based provider?

**Possible follow-ups:**
- What would the European alternative need to offer?
- Would slightly lower performance be acceptable for better privacy or control?

---

# 8. Budget and Capacity

### Question 20
How much time and money could the company realistically invest in testing a new AI tool?

**Possible follow-ups:**
- Would you prefer a small pilot first?
- Who would approve the spending?
- What level of subscription cost would require more consideration?

### Question 21
How would you decide whether an AI tool is worth paying for?

**Possible follow-ups:**
- time saved?
- better quality?
- more clients?
- less repetitive work?
- reduced outsourcing?

### Question 22
Would employees have enough time to learn a new AI workflow?

**Possible follow-ups:**
- Would training be necessary?
- Who would need the most training?

---

# 9. People and Internal Responsibility

### Question 23
If Reklaame decided to use AI more seriously, who should be responsible for it internally?

**Possible follow-ups:**
- management?
- one AI champion?
- individual employees?
- IT/external consultant?

### Question 24
How do employees currently feel about AI?

**Possible follow-ups:**
- excited?
- sceptical?
- worried about jobs?
- worried about creativity?
- already using AI independently?

### Question 25
Do you think employees might currently use AI without telling management or clients?

**Possible follow-ups:**
- Would that be a problem?
- Would company rules help?
- What should employees be required to disclose?

---

# 10. Prioritising AI Use Cases

### Question 26
If you could choose only one task to test with AI next month, which task would you choose?

**Possible follow-ups:**
- Why that task?
- What result would make the test successful?
- What could go wrong?

### Question 27
Which of these possible use cases would be most realistic for Reklaame?

Ask them to discuss, not simply rank:

- research and summarising;
- brainstorming campaign ideas;
- first drafts of copy;
- generating content variations;
- image or design ideation;
- summarising client briefs;
- internal documentation;
- reporting;
- administrative tasks.

### Question 28
Which of those use cases would you avoid completely?

**Follow-up:**
Why?

---

# 11. Measuring Success

### Question 29
If Reklaame tested an AI tool, how would you know whether the pilot was successful?

**Possible follow-ups:**
- less time spent?
- fewer repetitive tasks?
- same or better quality?
- lower cost?
- positive staff feedback?
- positive client feedback?

### Question 30
Would you be willing to compare the same task with and without AI to measure the difference?

**Possible follow-ups:**
- What task would be suitable?
- What would you measure?
- time?
- number of corrections?
- quality?
- cost?

---

# 12. Closing Questions

### Question 31
What is your biggest concern about using more AI in the company?

### Question 32
What is the biggest opportunity you think AI could create for Reklaame?

### Question 33
Is there anything about AI use in marketing agencies that you think we should have asked but did not?

### Question 34
Is there anything you would recommend we research further after this interview?

---

# After the Interview

Immediately after the interview, the team should record:

- the interview date;
- participant role;
- which questions were answered;
- important themes;
- surprising findings;
- claims that need external verification;
- unanswered questions;
- possible follow-up questions.

Do not treat one interview as evidence for the whole marketing sector.

Interview findings should be compared with desk research before broader conclusions are made.

Raw interview recordings, unredacted transcripts and confidential company information must not be sent to an unapproved AI or model service.
