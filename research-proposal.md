# AIBS Research Proposal — First Draft

## 1. Target Audience

This handbook is intended for small and medium-sized marketing and advertising agencies in Arnhem and within the Gelderland region, with approximately 10–50 employees.

These agencies provide services such as branding, digital marketing, graphic design, website development, advertising campaigns and content creation for other businesses.

Our initial example company is **Reklaame, based in Arnhem**. The company represents the type of creative SME that our research will focus on.

Marketing agencies are already exposed to generative AI tools such as ChatGPT, image-generation tools and AI-assisted design or content platforms. However, the important question for an agency owner is not simply which AI tools are available.

The real business decision is:

**Which parts of the agency's workflow should use AI to increase productivity, and which parts should remain primarily human in order to protect quality, originality and client trust?**

Our initial assumption is that AI could reduce the time employees spend on tasks such as research, brainstorming, first drafts of content, summarising client briefs and preparing variations of marketing materials. However, this assumption must be tested through desk research and a company visit.

## 2. What These SMEs Need to Know

Small marketing and advertising agencies need practical and real-time information that helps them decide whether AI adoption creates real business value.

The Draghi report highlights Europe's need to improve productivity, innovation and the adoption of digital technologies in order to remain competitive. For small businesses, however, adopting new technology can be tricky and this is because they have fewer financial and technical resources compared to large organisations.

For a small marketing agency, AI may offer opportunities to complete certain tasks more quickly, which eventually increases creative output without spending hefty amount of time and serve more clients without increasing staff at the same rate.

At the same time, AI creates several risks.

AI-generated information can be inaccurate. Generated content may be generic or inconsistent with a client's brand. Agencies also need to consider copyright, privacy, confidential client information, transparency and the effect of AI on employees' creative skills.

Therefore, the handbook should help an owner decide:

* where AI can create useful productivity improvements;
* which tasks should not be fully automated;
* what risks need to be controlled;
* which skills employees need;
* what costs and implementation effort are involved;
* and how an agency can test AI on a small scale before making a larger investment.

## 3. Research Question

### Main Research Question

**How can small marketing and advertising agencies in Arnhem and Gelderland use generative AI to improve productivity while maintaining creative quality, originality and client trust?**

### External Analysis Sub-questions

1. **Which current AI applications and developments are most relevant to small marketing and advertising agencies?**

2. **What external risks and requirements should marketing agencies consider when using generative AI, particularly regarding copyright, privacy, data protection, accuracy and responsible AI?**

3. **How is the increasing use of AI affecting competition, client expectations and the services offered by marketing agencies?**

### Internal Analysis Sub-questions

4. **Which activities in a small marketing agency currently require the most employee time and could potentially benefit from AI support?**

5. **What AI knowledge, digital capabilities, data and resources does the selected SME currently have?**

6. **Which potential AI use cases provide the best balance between business value, implementation effort, creative quality and risk for the selected SME?**

## 4. Quality Criteria for the Handbook

The handbook should be judged using the following criteria.

A good handbook page must:

* use clear English that a non-technical SME owner can understand;
* answer a real business decision rather than only explain AI technology;
* be based on reliable and relevant evidence;
* distinguish facts, research findings and assumptions;
* provide sources for important claims;
* include evidence relevant to small businesses and the regional context where possible;
* explain both opportunities and limitations of AI;
* discuss responsible-AI issues including privacy, copyright, accuracy and human oversight where relevant;
* provide practical examples that are realistic for a small marketing agency;
* explain expected business value rather than only describing AI features;
* avoid recommending AI where there is insufficient evidence that it would be useful;
* give the owner clear actions or questions to consider after reading the page;
* remain concise enough to be useful to a busy business owner.

These criteria will also be used to evaluate pages produced by the AEL platform.

## 5. Methodology

The research will use a combination of desk research and field research.

### Desk Research

Desk research will be used mainly to answer the external-analysis questions and provide background evidence for the internal analysis.

Sources may include:

* the Draghi report;
* European Commission publications;
* CBS;
* KVK;
* sector organisations;
* academic research;
* professional reports;
* reliable technology and business publications;
* Dutch regional business sources.

Desk research will investigate current AI applications in marketing, adoption trends, business opportunities, productivity effects, copyright, privacy, responsible AI and other external risks.

Both Dutch and English sources will be used.

Sources will be evaluated for authority, relevance, date, evidence quality and possible bias before being included in the handbook.

### Field Research

One SME visit will take place during weeks 2–4.

The preferred case company is a small marketing or advertising agency in Arnhem, with Reklaame as the initial target.

A semi-structured interview will be conducted with an owner, manager or employee who understands the company's workflows.

The interview will investigate:

* how work currently moves from a client brief to a finished output;
* which activities require the most employee time;
* whether AI is already being used;
* which AI tools employees currently know or use;
* problems or frustrations in the current workflow;
* attitudes towards AI-generated creative work;
* concerns about copyright, privacy and client information;
* employee skills and readiness;
* and which AI applications the company would realistically consider testing.

The field research primarily supports the internal-analysis questions.

The results of the interview will then be compared with the desk-research findings.

This comparison will help identify which AI opportunities are theoretically possible and which are actually relevant to the needs and capabilities of a small marketing agency.
