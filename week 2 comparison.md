# AIBS Assignment 1 — AI Article Comparison

All three articles agree that no single country or region completely dominates artificial intelligence. They consistently identify the United States as strongest in frontier AI models, private investment and computing infrastructure, while China is presented as particularly strong in research, patents and large-scale deployment, and Europe as stronger in regulation and responsible AI.

However, the articles use different definitions of AI leadership and therefore create different impressions of who is ahead. The US article describes the US as the current overall leader, the China article presents China and the US as leaders in different areas, while the Europe article argues that Europe is clearly behind both despite its regulatory and research strengths.

The articles also differ in the evidence they provide. The Europe article gives many precise statistics and names several sources, while the US and China articles make broad claims about investment, research, patents, models and infrastructure without directly citing evidence for them. Even where sources are mentioned, individual claims are not consistently connected to a specific source or methodology.

Comparing the three articles showed us that simply asking AI **"who leads AI?" is not a reliable research method**. The answer depends on how leadership is defined, which indicators the model chooses to emphasise and what evidence it uses, while the confident writing style can make unsupported or difficult-to-verify claims appear equally reliable.
