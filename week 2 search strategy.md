## Source Quality Criteria

For this research, a high-quality source should:

- have an identifiable author or organisation;
- come from a credible primary, academic or recognised research institution;
- provide evidence or explain the methodology behind its claims;
- be sufficiently recent for the rapidly changing AI sector;
- distinguish measured evidence from forecasts or opinions;
- disclose the geographical scope and time period of statistics;
- provide traceable data and references;
- be relevant to the US–China–Europe AI competition;
- avoid relying entirely on vendor claims;
- be cross-checkable against independent sources.
- ## Search Strategy

We searched for evidence on AI leadership across the United States,
China and Europe using several dimensions rather than treating
"AI leadership" as a single measure.

The search was divided into:

1. frontier AI models and model performance;
2. AI research and publications;
3. semiconductor and compute capacity;
4. private and public AI investment;
5. AI adoption and industrial deployment;
6. patents and innovation;
7. regulation and geopolitical dependencies.

We prioritised primary and institutional sources such as the
European Commission, Stanford AI Index, OECD, WIPO and official
statistics.

Searches were conducted using combinations such as:

"US China Europe AI investment 2025 2026"
"AI Index China US Europe models"
"China US AI patents WIPO"
"Europe AI adoption SMEs"
"AI compute US China Europe"
"AI research publications China United States Europe"

Where possible, important claims were checked against a second
independent source.
