# AI Use Cases for Small Marketing Agencies: AI-Assisted Content Creation

## Why This Matters

Small marketing and advertising agencies in the Netherlands often manage several clients simultaneously, producing social media posts, advertising copy, campaign concepts and other marketing materials. Creating content requires time, creativity and an understanding of each client's brand.

Generative AI offers opportunities to support these activities, particularly by producing first drafts and alternative versions of content. However, agencies need to understand whether AI actually improves productivity without reducing quality, originality or client trust.

This chapter examines one practical use case: **AI-assisted social media content creation**.

## 1. The Business Problem

A small marketing agency may need to create multiple social media posts for different clients every week. Each post requires understanding the client's objectives, identifying the target audience, developing appropriate messaging and reviewing the final content.

Some activities, such as drafting initial captions or creating alternative headlines, may be repetitive. At the same time, every client expects content that reflects their brand identity.

The business decision is therefore whether AI can reduce drafting time while maintaining quality and leaving creative decisions with employees.

This is a potential opportunity identified through desk research, not a confirmed problem at our selected SME. An interview would be needed to establish whether content drafting is a significant bottleneck.

## 2. How AI Could Be Used

A marketing employee could provide an AI tool with an approved campaign brief containing the target audience, tone of voice, communication objectives and relevant product information.

The AI could then generate several caption drafts, suggest alternative headlines or adapt existing content for different social media platforms.

The workflow would be:

**Approved client brief → AI-generated content drafts → Human editing → Brand and accuracy check → Client approval → Publication**

The AI would assist with preparing content rather than independently publishing material or making decisions on behalf of the client.

This approach allows employees to spend less time producing initial variations and potentially more time on strategy, creative direction and quality control.

## 3. What Information Does the AI Need?

The tool may require a campaign objective, intended audience, tone of voice, product description and examples of previously approved content.

However, the agency should not automatically provide confidential client information, unpublished strategies, customer databases or personal information to external AI services.

The Dutch Data Protection Authority emphasises that organisations must assess privacy and data-protection requirements when using generative AI.

For an initial pilot, the agency could use fictional examples or approved non-confidential material. This would allow employees to evaluate the tool without exposing sensitive information.

**Source appraisal:** The Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens is the official Dutch data-protection regulator and provides authoritative guidance on GDPR-related risks.

## 4. Expected Benefits and Limitations

The main potential benefit is reduced time spent preparing first drafts and content variations. AI may also help employees explore different creative directions and adapt messages to different audiences.

However, these benefits should not be assumed. AI-generated content can be generic, inaccurate or inconsistent with a client's brand. Employees may need additional time to correct factual mistakes, rewrite captions or check whether content meets campaign requirements.

Research on generative AI and productivity shows that performance improvements can depend on the task and the employee's experience. For example, Noy and Zhang (2023) found productivity improvements in controlled professional writing tasks, but these findings cannot automatically be applied to every marketing workflow.

**Source appraisal:** Noy and Zhang's peer-reviewed research provides experimental evidence about generative AI in writing tasks. Its limitation is that the study does not directly measure productivity in Dutch marketing agencies.

The agency therefore needs to test whether the expected benefits occur in its own working environment.

## 5. Risks and Responsible AI

Several risks need to be considered before introducing AI-assisted content creation.

**Accuracy:** AI may generate incorrect information about products, services or market conditions. Factual claims should be verified before publication.

**Brand consistency:** Generated content may not match the client's identity, tone of voice or creative expectations.

**Privacy:** Client information entered into an AI service may be processed outside the agency's direct control, depending on the provider and service configuration.

**Copyright and originality:** AI-generated content may raise questions about originality and intellectual-property rights. Agencies should review outputs and the provider's terms before using them commercially.

**Employee responsibility:** Staff should understand that AI output is a draft, not an automatically approved final product.

The European Commission's AI-literacy guidance also highlights the importance of ensuring that people using AI systems have an appropriate understanding of their capabilities and risks.

**Source appraisal:** The European Commission is the primary institutional source for the EU AI Act and related AI-literacy requirements.

## 6. How Could an SME Test This Use Case?

Instead of introducing AI across all content-production activities, the agency could run a small pilot.

For example, employees could prepare ten comparable social media captions using their normal process and ten using AI-assisted drafting. The agency should record the time required for drafting, editing and final approval in both cases.

The evaluation should consider:

| Indicator | What to measure |
|---|---|
| Productivity | Total time from brief to approved caption |
| Quality | Number of corrections and reviewer assessment |
| Brand consistency | Whether the content matches approved brand guidelines |
| Cost | Subscription, training and review effort |
| Employee experience | Whether AI improves or complicates the workflow |
| Client satisfaction | Feedback on approved final content, where available |

Because different briefs may vary in difficulty, the results should be treated as an initial operational test rather than proof that AI causes a specific productivity improvement.

A successful pilot would demonstrate measurable time savings without reducing quality, increasing confidentiality risks or creating excessive review work.

## 7. What Should the Owner Do?

Before adopting AI-assisted content creation, the agency owner should identify one suitable workflow, select an approved tool and establish clear rules for its use.

The owner should make sure employees understand what information may be entered into the system, who checks generated content and who approves the final material.

The most practical first step is to **test AI on a small number of low-risk content tasks and compare the results with the existing workflow**.

If AI saves time while maintaining creative quality, the agency can consider expanding its use. If the results require extensive corrections or create new risks, the workflow should be improved or the tool should not be adopted for that task.

The goal is not to automate creativity completely, but to determine where AI can support employees and create measurable business value.
