# AI Use Cases at SMEs: AI-Assisted Content Creation for Marketing Agencies

## Why This Matters

Small marketing and advertising agencies in the Netherlands often work with multiple clients and need to produce social media posts, advertising copy, campaign concepts and other creative materials. These activities require time, creativity and an understanding of each client's brand.

Generative AI offers opportunities to support this work by producing first drafts, suggesting creative ideas and generating alternative versions of content. However, using AI does not automatically improve productivity or creative quality.

Research by Noy and Zhang (2023), published in *Science*, found that participants using ChatGPT completed professional writing tasks approximately 40% faster, while the quality of their output increased by 18%. Although these findings demonstrate potential productivity benefits, they cannot automatically be applied to Dutch marketing agencies.

The important question for an SME owner is therefore:

**Can AI reduce the time required to produce marketing content while maintaining originality, accuracy and client trust?**

## 1. The Business Problem

Marketing agencies regularly prepare content for different audiences, brands and communication channels. A single campaign may require multiple social media captions, advertising headlines and variations of promotional messages.

Preparing these materials can involve repetitive drafting and editing. At the same time, the final content must reflect the client's brand identity and communication objectives.

AI-assisted content creation could help employees produce initial drafts more efficiently, allowing them to focus on creative direction, strategy and client relationships.

However, this is a proposed opportunity rather than a confirmed problem at our selected SME. Field research is necessary to determine whether content production is a significant bottleneck and whether employees would benefit from AI assistance.

## 2. How AI Could Be Used

The proposed use case is **AI-assisted social media content creation**.

A marketing employee provides an approved campaign brief containing the target audience, communication objectives, product information and brand tone of voice.

The AI tool generates several draft captions or alternative headlines. The employee then evaluates the suggestions, edits the content and checks whether it meets the client's requirements.

The workflow would be:

**Approved Brief → AI Draft Generation → Human Editing → Accuracy and Brand Review → Client Approval → Publication**

For example, an agency preparing a social media campaign for a local restaurant could use AI to generate five different captions promoting a new menu. Employees would select and improve the strongest options before presenting them to the client.

The AI would support content creation but would not independently publish material or make decisions on behalf of the client.

## 3. What Information Does AI Need?

The system requires information such as the campaign objective, intended audience, tone of voice, product description and examples of approved content.

However, agencies must carefully consider which information they provide to external AI services.

Client databases, confidential campaign strategies, unpublished business information and personal data should not automatically be entered into AI tools.

The Dutch Data Protection Authority (Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens, 2026) provides guidance for organisations implementing generative AI. It recommends evaluating whether an organisation wants, is able and is legally permitted to use generative AI, particularly when the technology accesses existing organisational information.

For an initial pilot, agencies should use fictional or approved non-confidential material whenever possible.

**Source appraisal:** The Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens is the independent Dutch data-protection regulator and provides primary guidance on GDPR-related risks and responsibilities.

## 4. Expected Benefits and Limitations

The main expected benefit is a reduction in time spent preparing first drafts and creating content variations.

AI may also help employees explore alternative creative approaches and adapt messaging for different audiences.

The study by Noy and Zhang (2023) provides experimental evidence that generative AI can improve productivity in professional writing tasks. However, the study did not specifically investigate Dutch marketing agencies or social media campaigns.

Therefore, the productivity improvements observed in that research should not be treated as guaranteed results for this use case.

AI-generated content may also be inaccurate, repetitive, generic or inconsistent with a client's brand. Employees may need additional time to correct mistakes or rewrite the output.

For this reason, the agency should evaluate the **complete workflow**, including drafting, editing and approval, rather than measuring only how quickly AI generates text.

## 5. Risks and Responsible AI

Responsible AI use is especially important when marketing content is created for clients.

**Accuracy:** AI may generate incorrect information about products, services or promotional offers. Employees must verify factual claims before publication.

**Brand consistency:** AI-generated content may not match a client's tone of voice or creative expectations. Human review remains necessary.

**Privacy:** Information entered into an external AI service may be processed by a third-party provider. Agencies must review the provider's data-handling practices and ensure that personal-data processing complies with applicable requirements.

**Copyright and originality:** Agencies should review generated content for originality and possible intellectual-property concerns, particularly when using AI-generated images or creative assets.

**Employee skills:** Employees need to understand the capabilities and limitations of the AI tools they use.

Article 4 of the EU AI Act requires providers and deployers of AI systems to take measures supporting AI literacy among relevant staff and other people operating AI systems on their behalf.

**Source appraisal:** The European Commission is a primary institutional source for understanding the EU AI Act and AI-literacy requirements.

## 6. How Could the SME Test This Use Case?

Before implementing AI across the agency, the owner should conduct a small pilot.

For example, the agency could select ten comparable social media content tasks and complete each task using both the existing manual process and an AI-assisted process, with the order balanced to reduce learning effects.

The agency should record the total time required for drafting, editing and approval, together with the quality of the final content.

| Evaluation criterion | Measurement |
|---|---|
| Productivity | Total time from brief to approved content |
| Quality | Number of corrections and reviewer assessment |
| Brand consistency | Alignment with client brand guidelines |
| Cost | Subscription, training and employee review time |
| Employee experience | Feedback from staff using the tool |
| Client satisfaction | Feedback on approved content, where available |

A successful pilot would show that AI reduces the total time required without lowering content quality or introducing unacceptable risks.

The pilot results should be interpreted cautiously because a small sample cannot establish that the same benefits will occur across every client or campaign.

## 7. Practical Recommendation for the Owner

The agency should begin with one repetitive, relatively low-risk task, such as preparing initial social media captions using approved, non-confidential campaign information.

Before starting, the owner should establish who is allowed to use the tool, which information may be entered, who checks the output and who approves the final content.

The agency should then compare AI-assisted work with its existing process using measurable criteria.

If the pilot demonstrates meaningful time savings while maintaining creative quality and client trust, the agency can consider expanding the use of AI to similar tasks.

If the results require extensive corrections or introduce new risks, the workflow should be improved or the tool should not be adopted for that activity.

**The goal is not to replace human creativity, but to identify where AI can support employees and create measurable business value.**
